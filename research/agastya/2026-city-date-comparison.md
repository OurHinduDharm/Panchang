# Drik Agastya Arghya: 2026 city-date comparison

**Captured/reviewed:** 14 November 2026  
**Purpose:** preserve same-provider city sensitivity as context for the Pithoragarh traditional Agastya Arghya series.  
**Scope:** calendar festival dates are not equivalent to a coordinate-exact naked-eye Canopus first-visibility event.

The seven city labels span 3–10 September 2026. This is strong evidence that the displayed Drik date is location-dependent. It is **not** independent consensus: all rows come from the same provider. It is also not proof of the exact coordinates/elevation used by those pages.

Only the Moradabad and Pithoragarh rows in this comparison were retrieved from direct Agastya Arghya date/time pages with raw Tara-rise clock notation. The remaining rows are calendar-level festival dates; their exact Tara-rise clock must not be inferred from the date.

## Project decision

1. Keep Pithoragarh's public traditional-Arghya series separate and location-specific.
2. Do not average the city dates, copy a nearby city's time, or use New Delhi as a fallback.
3. Do not treat a calendar-only festival date as an exact rise-time measurement.
4. Keep traditional Agastya Arghya separate from modern Canopus naked-eye heliacal first visibility and geometric horizon rise.
5. No existing public date/time or main Panchang output is changed by this comparison.
