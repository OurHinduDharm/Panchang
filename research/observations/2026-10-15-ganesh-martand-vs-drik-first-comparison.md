# First comparison: Ganesh Martand 2026–27 vs Drik published Asta/Udaya dates

**Date recorded:** 2026-10-15  
**Purpose:** Compare the project owner's transcribed Ganesh Martand traditional reference with published Drik Panchang dates without conflating event definitions.

## Primary traditional reference

Ganesh Martand Panchang, 2026–27 Raudra Samvatsara issue; source transcription preserved in [2026-10-15-ganesh-martand-udaya-asta-reference.md](2026-10-15-ganesh-martand-udaya-asta-reference.md).

- Venus: eastern setting 12 Oct 2026, 17:40; western rising 29 Oct 2026, 06:26.
- Jupiter: setting 15 Jul 2026, 19:09; rising 10 Aug 2026, 05:40.
- Times are recorded as local clock times in HH:MM notation. The Panchang day is sunrise-to-next-sunrise; do not convert the listed time into elapsed time since sunrise.

## Published comparison

### Venus, 2026

The existing Drik Panchang location-specific page for Deoranian, Uttar Pradesh reports:
- Asta starts: 12 Oct 2026, 18:10.
- Asta ends: 29 Oct 2026, 05:51.

Comparison with Ganesh Martand:
- Asta-start civil date matches; times differ by 30 minutes.
- End/Udaya civil date matches; times differ by 35 minutes.
- This is **strong date-level corroboration for these two Venus events**, but it does not establish that the source algorithms or event definitions are identical. Drik states that its displayed combustion dates use modern algorithms; its page separately mentions approximate Sūrya-siddhānta angular criteria.

Source: https://www.drikpanchang.com/planet/asta/shukra-asta-date-time.html?year=2026

### Jupiter, 2026

Drik Panchang's 2026 Manmad page reports:
- Asta starts: 17 Jul 2026, 19:48.
- Asta ends: 11 Aug 2026, 05:30.

Its 2026 Alibag page reports:
- Asta starts: 17 Jul 2026, 19:52.
- Asta ends: 10 Aug 2026, 29:39+, which normalizes to 11 Aug 2026, 05:39.

Comparison with Ganesh Martand:
- Asta-start date differs by two civil dates (Ganesh Martand 15 Jul vs Drik 17 Jul).
- Udaya/end is close in clock time but differs in displayed date: Ganesh Martand 10 Aug, 05:40 vs Drik's normalized 11 Aug, around 05:30–05:39. The approximately one-day date discrepancy must not be silently normalized away.
- Drik's own location-specific end times differ by minutes, while the date-label discrepancy against Ganesh Martand is larger. Before deciding whether this is a sunrise-day attribution convention, a traditional-vs-modern event definition, or a true calculation difference, we need to inspect the original printed page and establish the intended local location/time convention.
- Therefore the Jupiter result is **partial agreement in broad window, not a confirmed exact match**.

Sources:
- https://www.drikpanchang.com/planet/asta/guru-asta-date-time.html?lang=en
- https://www.drikpanchang.com/planet/asta/guru-asta-date-time.html?time-format=24plushour

## Decision for OurHinduDharm research

1. Preserve Ganesh Martand's printed date/time as the primary traditional benchmark.
2. Record Venus as a date-level match with modest clock-time differences.
3. Record Jupiter as a discrepancy requiring event/date convention review; do not replace Ganesh Martand's dates with Drik dates.
4. Keep TKS Shāstrīya results, Drik's modern location-specific combustion results, independent Chapter IX reconstruction, and scientific heliacal visibility in separate columns.
5. Use the repeated Mercury events as additional fixtures to test the event labels, sequence, time convention, and date attribution.
6. No production files or calculations were changed by this comparison.
