# JPL Horizons cross-check: Pithoragarh planetary Asta/Udaya (2021–2027)

Generated from the independent JPL Horizons observer-ephemeris run on 2026-11-10.

## Scope

- Reference: 75 accepted direct Drik Pithoragarh windows, covering 2021–2027.
- Boundaries checked: 150 Asta-start/Asta-end timestamps across Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn.
- Independent ephemeris: JPL Horizons, DE441, topocentric observer output.
- Observer proxy: latitude 29.5828° N, longitude 80.2182° E, altitude 1.65 km.
- JPL request: geodetic site, apparent airless AZ/EL, UT timestamps, target apparent magnitude and illuminated fraction.
- Samples: published timestamp and ±10 minutes; local -0.5° altitude crossing linearly interpolated from the surrounding samples.

## Interpretation and limitations

1. The independent geometry is strongly consistent with the published Drik boundary timestamps representing a local planetary horizon-crossing condition near -0.5° for these 150 samples.
2. This is a cross-check of event geometry, not proof of Drik's complete internal visibility algorithm and not a fit/training dataset.
3. Solar altitude differs substantially by planet and event. A single universal solar-depression threshold is not supported by these observations.
4. The project elevation of 1650 m may be orthometric height above mean sea level, whereas Horizons geodetic site altitude is ellipsoidal. The 1.65 km input is therefore an observer proxy until the geoid conversion is established.
5. Horizons was queried with AIRLESS apparent coordinates. Atmospheric refraction, local terrain/horizon profile, extinction, weather and actual naked-eye detectability are not validated by this run.
6. Drik's internal observer coordinates and elevation are not published. The comparison is by its Pithoragarh city label against the project's named coordinate.
7. The report covers the five planets only; it does not replace the existing daily Sun/Moon horizon-rise and set calculations.
